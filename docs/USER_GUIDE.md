# User Guide
> [!NOTE]
> This guide is under development. Current sections provide an overview of each screen; step-by-step
> instructions will be added as the app approaches release.

This guide explains Budget Brewer from the user's point of view.

## Home

The Home screen gives you a bird's-eye view of your finances.

It includes dashboard cards and charts for:

- income versus expenses;
- expenses breakdown;
- savings comparison;
- spending trends;
- spending by tag.

The Home screen is read-only. It is meant for observing, comparing, and moving
to the area that needs attention.

The timeframe buttons let you review spending trends across different ranges.

## Finances

The Finances screen is where the monthly budget is built.

Use it to enter:

- income;
- tips or miscellaneous income;
- expense categories;
- expenses;
- savings and spending allocations.

This is the screen where "every penny has a purpose" becomes concrete.

After income and expenses are entered, Budget Brewer shows what is left. That
leftover amount can be allocated toward savings, spending, or both.

## Savings

Savings buckets are dedicated places for money you want to keep building over
time.

There are two bucket types:

- Goal buckets, which have a target amount.
- Growth buckets, which do not require a target.

Savings buckets can receive distributions from the available savings pool. You
can also deduct from a bucket or withdraw/archive a bucket when it is no longer
active.

Bucket history keeps a transaction record so savings changes are not just a
single number with no memory behind it.

## Expenses

The Expenses screen gives you a daily checklist of monthly expenses.

Use it to review what is due and mark expenses as paid.

Recurring expenses are part of the monthly planning flow and should remain easy
to recognize during review.

## Spending

The Spending screen tracks purchases against the spending allowance you created
from the Finances screen.

Use it to:

- add spending transactions;
- review spending by date;
- see remaining spending allowance;
- notice when a purchase would exceed the remaining amount.

The point is not to create dozens of tiny micro-budgets. The point is to know
whether today's spending still fits inside the money you intentionally set aside.

## Calendar

The Calendar screen shows budgeting activity across the month.

It can show activity such as:

- expenses;
- spending;
- income;
- savings.

Tap a day to review that day's details.

The calendar is useful for checking timing, spotting clusters of activity, and
understanding how the month is flowing.

## Settings

The Settings screen contains account, appearance, privacy, export, currency, and
archive-related options.

Depending on the current app state, Settings may include:

- sign in or sign up;
- sign out;
- password reset or verification flows;
- export current month data;
- review past months;
- PIN or biometric lock;
- theme selection;
- currency selection;
- archive access;
- account deletion.

## Accounts And Sync

A Budget Brewer account is optional for the core local budgeting experience.

Users who create an account can use cloud-backed features intended to make data
available across devices and provide additional recovery convenience.

The app follows an offline-first model, so day-to-day budgeting data is stored
locally and should remain usable when the device is offline.

## Export

Export features are currently available through the account-related Settings
flow.

Budget Brewer supports CSV and PDF export for supported budgeting data.

Broader local-only export and import support is planned so users can back up,
move, or review their data without requiring a cloud account.

## Privacy Lock

PIN and biometric privacy-lock features are currently available through the
account-related Settings flow.

A future update may make local privacy-lock features available without requiring
an account, supporting a fully local budgeting experience.

## Planned Local-Only Data Tools

Budget Brewer is intended to support a complete local-only experience.

Planned improvements include:

- exporting data without requiring an account;
- importing local backup data;
- moving data between devices through user-controlled files;
- using PIN or biometric protection without cloud sync;

An account would remain optional and would primarily provide the convenience of
automatic synchronization and cloud-backed availability.
